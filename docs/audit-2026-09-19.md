# Mission X — audit before the polish sweep (19 Sep 2026)

Scope: commit `bba44cc`. Method: read every client file against a game-feel checklist; measured the running build
(draw calls, triangles, canvas resolution, DOM labels) at phone size; measured the data (booth, avatar, aisle sizes,
multi-booth stands); checked input, layout, meta and failure paths in code. Nothing here was tested on a real phone or
a real deployment — that absence is itself finding S1.

## Measured

- Booth: 2.82 × 3.06 × 2.6 m (true shell-scheme size). Astronaut at game scale: **2.85 m tall, helmet 1.4 m wide** —
  taller than a booth, half a booth wide. Seated crowd and chairs are at 1.0× → a seated player overflows the chair
  and dwarfs the people next to them.
- 323 exhibitors; **116 have more than one booth (420 booths)**, the largest 26. Each is drawn as separate identical
  cubes, so a 26-booth island stand reads as 26 small booths.
- Frame at the entrance, phone size, 2× density: **219 draw calls, ~219,000 triangles, 64 HTML labels restyled every
  frame**. Every character is 18 separate meshes: with 24 players nearby draw calls pass ~600. All three levels are
  always submitted to the GPU (culling is off on the big meshes), whichever level you are on.
- Download: 524 KB engine (136 KB gzipped) + 77 KB game + 149 KB floor data. Fine on 4G.
- `:hover` rules in the stylesheet: **0**. Escape / Back-button handling: **none**. Social preview image, favicon,
  home-screen icon: **none**. Sound, haptics: **none**.

## Findings

### S1 — blocks calling it "stable" (do first)
1. **Never run on a real phone or a real deployment.** Touch, GPU speed on a cheap Android, iOS Safari quirks, and the
   in-app browsers of Instagram / Facebook / TikTok — where ad traffic will open it — are all unknown.
2. **Presence will not scale as deployed.** Each player pings every 2 s and each ping is ~5 database round trips on the
   serverless setup. A few hundred concurrent players would exhaust a small Turso plan. Needs a memory store
   (Redis/Upstash) or a slower, smarter ping before any ad spend.
3. **Tap-to-walk is dead in the lower-left of a phone screen** (left 50 % × lower 55 %): any touch there spawns the
   joystick, so a tap never becomes "walk there". That is where thumbs land.
4. **The phone's Back button leaves the game** instead of closing the open sheet; Escape does nothing on desktop.
5. **Typing a contact note walks your astronaut** (W/A/S/D are not ignored in text areas), and the keyboard still
   moves you while any sheet is open.

### S2 — visible roughness
6. **Other players move in a stop-go rhythm**: their position arrives every 2 s and is eased toward, so they
   accelerate and brake with every update. Needs proper snapshot interpolation (constant speed between updates).
7. **Toasts pile up over the objective card** (hall card + place card + points at once can cover "Take me there").
   Games queue these: one at a time.
8. **Scale mismatch** (see Measured): pick one character scale and size furniture, seats and crowd to it.
9. **Sitting teleports you** to the nearest free seat (can be many metres away) instead of walking to it; two real
   players can sit on the same chair.
10. **Photo capture uses an async bitmap read of the 3D canvas** — worked in Chromium, a known risk on iOS Safari
    (can come out blank). Should read pixels synchronously.
11. **Below the slab is empty paper**: at the entrance a third of a phone screen is blank. The building needs ground
    around it.
12. **The expression tray** stays open until you pick something; tapping elsewhere should close it.

### S3 — what stops it feeling like a finished game
13. **No hover, no cursor, no click-to-select on desktop.** Buttons do not react under the mouse; booths cannot be
    clicked to open them (you must walk next to one and press a chip). Hover-highlight + click → booth sheet is the
    most natural interaction and is missing.
14. **Silent.** No footsteps, no stamp sound, no UI ticks, no ambience, no vibration. Sound is half of game feel.
15. **No first-ten-seconds moment.** The first screen is calm and static. No sweep over the venue, no "38 people here
    now", no preview of the card you will get, prize still unnamed.
16. **Nobody is taught the controls.** Drag-to-look, pinch-to-zoom, the joystick, tap-to-walk: none is shown.
17. **Camera never avoids obstacles**: characters are as tall as booths, so from some angles your astronaut is hidden
    behind a row. Needs occlusion fade or a slightly higher default pitch.
18. **Exhibitor presence is weak**: no name on the booth itself (only a floating label when near), no logo, merged
    stands not shown as one. "That is MY booth in the game" is the exhibitor's wow and it is not there yet.
19. **Interface does not scale up** on large monitors / TVs: fixed 15 px base, cards stay phone-sized on a 27″ screen.

### S4 — accuracy notes
20. Level 2 is stretched ~9 % north–south from the source PDF (3.27 m row pitch drawn vs 3.00 m real).
21. Levels 1 and 3 have booths, halls, lifts and places but no walls or doors.
22. The three levels lie side by side, not stacked; the lift "ride" crosses sideways. Stacking them would be truer
    and a stronger picture of MITEC.
23. Booth 8H18B: the organiser's plan prints another name on that cell, and it is mid-row while the booth render
    shows a corner. Still to be confirmed with the organiser — the whole game points at that spot.
24. Run speed is 6.5 m/s (a sprint). Right for game pace; say so rather than call it realistic.

### S5 — product and business
25. **Unofficial use of the MIHAS name, floor plan and exhibitor names.** The disclaimer is there; permission is not.
    Before paid promotion this needs the organiser's nod, or a fallback (generic hall names, exhibitors shown only
    after they opt in).
26. Privacy notice is still an unreviewed draft; company details are placeholders.
27. No error monitoring, no analytics dashboard, no uptime check, no CI. After launch nobody would know it broke.
28. **Monetisation (audit only):** today this is a strong pilot, not a product. What could be sold later: (a) the
    engine as a white-label "playable floor plan + consented lead capture" for other expos; (b) sponsored placement
    inside it — booth of the day, named stage or café, featured on the map; (c) an exhibitor tier — logo on the
    booth, lead export, visit analytics. All three need one real event's numbers (players, completion rate, leads per
    exhibitor) and the organiser relationship first.

## What is good and should not be touched
One-page rules; one palette with meaning; the objective card / thumb dock layout; places generated from the real plan;
facts counted from data; consent-by-field card sharing; server-authoritative points with an auditable history; demo
mode. The foundation is sound — the gaps are feel, input edge cases, and proof under real conditions.

## Proposed sweep (in this order)
1. **Input and navigation correctness** — tap vs joystick, Back/Esc closes sheets, keyboard guards, tray dismiss,
   toast queue, walk-to-seat, seat reservation.
2. **Scale and composition** — one character scale; furniture and crowd to match; ground around the building;
   merged multi-booth stands with the exhibitor's name on the fascia; camera occlusion.
3. **Motion** — snapshot interpolation for other players; eased camera; stamp / arrive micro-animations.
4. **Desktop feel** — hover states, cursors, booth hover-highlight and click-to-open, tooltips, UI scale on large screens.
5. **Sound and haptics** — small, tasteful set, mute button, off by default until first tap.
6. **Performance** — per-level culling, single-mesh characters, label updates only on change, crowd LOD; then
   measure again.
7. **First ten seconds** — opening sweep, live count, card preview, control hints; social preview image and icons.
8. **Proof** — real phones (one cheap Android, one iPhone, Instagram in-app browser), a real Vercel deploy, a load
   test of presence. Only after this can "stable" be said honestly.
